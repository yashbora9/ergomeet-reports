Part 1 · 11:01–11:37

Meeting Purpose

To learn the EVA framework for handling objections and skepticism.

Key Takeaways

  • The EVA Framework: A 3-step process (Empathy, Questions, Conclusion) to handle objections by understanding their root cause (hidden fear/wish) and guiding the person to their own solution.
  • Empathy First: Validate their perspective ("I understand why you'd think that") to prevent arguments. A won argument is a lost client.
  • Questions Over Arguments: Use questions to uncover the objection's root cause and lead the person to their own conclusion. Arguments close the mind; questions open it.
  • 80% of People Are Undecided: The EVA framework targets this group, shifting them from "maybe" to "yes" and potentially increasing your success rate to 90%.

Topics

The Problem: Objections & Skepticism

  • Newcomers face skepticism from their network, often framed as "pyramid scheme" or "scam."
  • Psychological Root: People often try to pull others down to their own level rather than rise with them, creating resistance to change.
  • Core Issue: Objections signal a lack of either trust or priority.
  • "Pyramid scheme" → Lack of trust.
  • "No time" → Lack of priority.

The Solution: The EVA Framework

  • A 3-step process to handle objections non-confrontationally.
  • E → Empathy:
  • Validate their perspective ("I understand why you'd think that").
  • Why: Prevents arguments. A won argument is a lost client.
  • Insight: Objections are hidden fears or wishes.
  • V → Questions (Fragen):
  • Uncover the objection's root cause.
  • Why: Arguments close the mind; questions open it.
  • Technique: Ask clarifying questions ("What do you mean by 'pyramid scheme'?") and listen actively.
  • A → Conclusion (Abschluss):
  • Guide them to their own conclusion, which is more powerful than being told.

Context: Adrian's Journey

  • Adrian joined the business at 23 while studying Economics & Politics.
  • Motivation: A desire to teach and interact with motivated people, a contrast to his previous tutoring experience.
  • Catalyst: A chance meeting with a schoolmate (Boris) and his leader (Jerry) led to the opportunity.
  • Decision Point: After 5 months, Adrian committed full-time, driven by early success (e.g., earning a five-figure monthly income).

Next Steps

  • Practice the EVA framework in all interactions, not just business.
  • Identify the root cause (fear or wish) behind each objection.
  • Use questions to guide people to their own conclusions.

Meeting Purpose

Mastering the EFA framework for handling objections and closing deals.

Key Takeaways

  • The EFA Framework: A three-step process for handling objections: Empathy (build trust), Fragen (use questions to guide self-realization), and Abschluss (summarize and close).
  • Master the 10–13 Core Objections: All objections stem from either "no trust" or "no priority." Learning the pre-written scripts for these takes only 4–6 hours and makes you an expert, enabling you to anticipate and counter any argument.
  • Proactive Learning is Your Responsibility: Your sponsor's success depends on yours. Proactively request the EFA scripts, learn them independently, and use practice sessions for refinement, not initial study.

Topics

The EFA Framework

  • Empathy (E): Build trust by genuinely understanding the prospect's perspective.
  • Why: Sincere empathy is critical for building rapport; overusing phrases like "I understand" sounds disingenuous.
  • How: Put yourself in their shoes and respond authentically.
  • Fragen (F): Use questions to guide prospects to their own conclusions.
  • Open Questions: Use early to gather information and encourage the prospect to open up.
  • Closed Questions: Use later to build a "yes-chain" that leads to a final agreement (e.g., setting an appointment).
  • Abschluss (A): Summarize the conversation and close the deal.
  • Why: Many reps fail at this final step despite excellent preparation.
  • How: Use "options questions" (e.g., "Would next Tuesday or Wednesday work better?") to make the final decision easier.

Mastering Objections

  • The Core Problem: All objections stem from one of two root causes:
  • No Trust: "Pyramid scheme," "scam," "you're too young."
  • No Priority: "No money," "no time," "not interested."
  • The Solution: Learn the pre-written EFA scripts for the 10–13 common objections.
  • Why: This makes you an expert, allowing you to anticipate and counter any argument.
  • How:
  • Learn: Independently study the scripts (4–6 hours).
  • Practice: Use sponsor sessions for refinement, not initial learning.

Part 3 · 12:38–13:06

Meeting Purpose

Mastering objection handling (EVA) and appointment setting techniques.

Key Takeaways

  • Use the "Patrick Star" technique to isolate objections with "Mal abgesehen davon..." (Aside from that...), secure a "yes" on the core value proposition, and then resolve the objection from a position of agreement.
  • Counter the "pyramid scheme" objection by guiding prospects to compare the business model to a standard corporate hierarchy. This lets them discover the similarity themselves, making the objection self-defeating.
  • Uncover hidden objections with the "Hand aufs Herz" (Real Talk) technique. This separates superficial excuses like "no time" from the real concerns that are blocking the appointment.
  • Set appointments using "Optionsfragen" (Option Questions). This gives prospects a choice, making them feel in control while you guide the conversation toward a confirmed time.

Topics

The EVA Framework: Empathy & Questioning

  • Empathy: Start with phrases like "Ich kann das komplett nachvollziehen" (I completely understand). This disarms defensiveness and builds rapport.
  • Questioning: Use questions to uncover the true objection and guide the prospect to their own solution.
  • Wunschtechnik (Wish Technique): Ask, "Was müsste ich Ihnen bieten, damit Sie zufrieden aus dem Gespräch gehen?" (What would I need to offer for you to leave satisfied?). This shifts their mindset to the meeting itself.
  • Katastrophentechnik (Catastrophe Technique): A last resort for stalled conversations: "Was kann im schlimmsten Fall passieren?" (What's the worst that could happen?). The answer is usually just 30 minutes of their time.

The "Pyramid Scheme" Objection

  • Problem: Directly arguing is a losing battle that creates confrontation.
  • Solution: Use a question-based sequence to help the prospect discover the answer themselves.

1. Define: "Was ist für Sie ein Schneeballsystem?" (What is a pyramid scheme to you?)

2. Relate: Ask about their job and their boss's income.

3. Compare: Guide them to see the similarity between their workplace hierarchy and the business model.

4. Conclude: Ask if their job is a pyramid scheme. A "no" makes their original objection self-defeating.

Appointment Setting: Optionsfragen

  • Problem: Direct questions ("Passt es Ihnen am Samstag um 14 Uhr?") invite a simple "yes" or "no," making it easy to get stuck in a loop of rejections.
  • Solution: Offer two options at each step to guide the prospect to a time they choose.
  • "Passt es Ihnen besser Anfang oder Ende der Woche?" (Beginning or end of the week?)
  • "Montag oder Dienstag?"
  • "Vormittags oder Nachmittags?"
  • Rationale: This gives the prospect a feeling of control, increasing the likelihood of a confirmed appointment.

Next Steps

  • All: Obtain objection-handling scripts from your "Schucki" (mentor).
  • All: Invest 4–6 hours in mastering these techniques, as they account for over 50% of success when combined with product knowledge.
